Say it with light.

ViCAS lets a driver send a spoken warning to the car ahead. An LED flickers too fast to see, and a solar panel on the other car turns that light back into a voice.

A horn can only say look at me.

It can’t say

“Your boot is open.” “Your luggage is slipping.” “Someone needs a hospital.” “Your fuel is low.”

Stopping to signal is dangerous, and hand gestures get missed. At Indore’s city bus control centre I was told drivers have no direct way to warn each other: they stop the bus, or call the centre and wait.

From key to voice.

Five parts, one beam of light. Scroll and follow the signal.

Key

Press a key

Nine keys, nine messages. Key 1 is “Your trunk is open”, 2 “There is a medical emergency”, 3 “Your fuel is low”, 4 “Someone is in danger”.

Brain

Arduino reads the SD card

An Arduino Uno finds that key’s recording, an 8-bit 16 kHz WAV file, and plays it on pin 9 with the TMRpcm library.

Light

The LED carries the voice

Through an amplifier, the audio drives a super-bright white LED. Its brightness follows the voice thousands of times a second, so the eye sees steady light.

Ear

A solar panel listens

On the car ahead, solar panels turn the flicker back into a voltage that follows the light, which rebuilds the audio signal.

Voice

The speaker says it

An LM386 amplifier lifts the signal through a 3.5 mm jack and the message plays. The receiving car needs no microcontroller.

Press a key. Hear it arrive.

This is the ViCAS keypad. Press a key and watch its message leave the LED as light, cross the gap and come out of the speaker on the other side.

Key 1 sends “Your trunk is open.” Press it to send.

Sender code Li-Fi code

Every key is eleven flashes.

In digital mode the key itself travels as data: a dark start bit, eight data bits least significant first, then the light comes back on. At 10 ms a bit, a key crosses the gap in 110 ms.

Key 1 0x31 00110001

    Why light, not radio?

    RadioMicrowaveInfraredVisibleLaser

    Radio

    Spreads in every direction and passes through walls, so everyone nearby can pick it up. Crowded bands mean interference, and some need a licence.

    Not private

    Microwave

    Travels in a line, but the equipment is costly, bulky and power-hungry for a car.

    Too costly

    Infrared

    Cheap and private, but short-ranged and easily blocked. It’s built for TV remotes, not traffic.

    Too short

    Visible light

    Reaches only the car it’s pointed at, needs no licence, adds nothing to the radio bands, and cars already carry LEDs. The whole ViCAS prototype cost ₹1,874.

    ViCAS uses this

    Laser

    Very private, but a beam strong enough to reach the next car can harm eyes and heat surfaces.

    Unsafe

    Ten feet. Fog, rain, even mud.

    I tested the link at 0, 2, 5, 7 and 10 feet in clear air, then through steam, sprayed water and paint sprayed over part of the panel. The message played every time. Try the conditions.
