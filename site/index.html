Visible-light Communication Audio System

Talk to the car ahead. With light.

ViCAS turns a spoken warning into a flicker too fast to see. A solar panel on the vehicle in front turns it back into a voice. No radio, no SIM, no Wi‑Fi. ₹1,874 in parts.

01 · The problem

A horn has
one word.

It's look at me. Everything else a driver needs to say gets lost.

  • Your boot is open.
  • Your luggage is slipping off the roof.
  • A child's hand is out of the window.
  • We have a medical emergency.

Try saying any of these with a horn.

What drivers do today

Dangerous

Stop and tell them

Pulling over in traffic to walk up to another vehicle puts both drivers at risk.

Missed

Wave and point

Hand signals through two windscreens are easy to misread and easier to miss.

Too slow

Call a control room

Indore's city buses have to phone the control centre, which then calls the other bus.

“There is no exact way for the drivers to communicate. They can only do so by stopping the bus.”

Mr. Roshan Agrawal, Director, Chartered Speed, at AICTSL's Integrated Command and Control Centre, Indore

And with driverless cars arriving on public roads, there's a new gap: a human driver has no way to tell a driverless car exactly what's wrong, and it has no way to tell them.

02 · How it works

Voice in. Light across.
Voice out.

One recording changes shape five times on its way to the car ahead. Watch the signal along the bottom as you scroll.

01 · Keypad

Press a key

A 4×4 keypad on the dashboard. Each key is one recorded message: 1 is “Your trunk is open”, 2 “There is a medical emergency”, 3 “Your fuel is low”, 4 “Someone is in danger”.

4×4 membrane keypad · Keypad.h

02 · SD card

Find the recording

The Arduino opens 1.WAV on a micro SD card over SPI. It's a pre-recorded voice, stored as 8-bit samples, 16,000 of them for every second of speech.

Micro SD reader · SPI · CS on pin 10

03 · Arduino Uno

Turn it into pulses

The TMRpcm library plays the file on pin 9. It switches the pin on and off thousands of times a second, and the width of each pulse carries how loud the voice is at that instant.

TMRpcm · Timer1 PWM · pin 9

04 · LED

Flicker faster than eyes

A super-bright white LED follows those pulses. To anyone watching it just looks lit. Its brightness is actually rising and falling with the voice, too fast to see.

Super-bright white LED · line of sight

05 · Solar panels

Catch the light

On the back of the vehicle in front, four small solar panels are the sensor. Their voltage rises and falls with the light hitting them, so the voice comes back out as an electrical signal.

4 solar panels · no microcontroller needed

06 · Amplifier + speaker

Say it out loud

A 3.5 mm jack carries that signal into an LM386 amplifier, which drives the speaker. The driver ahead hears it: “Your trunk is open.”

LM386 module · 3.5 mm jack · speaker

Fog, rain, mud.

I set the LED and the panel up on a bench, then moved the panel back to 0, 2, 5, 7 and 10 feet and checked that the speaker played. Then I did it all again through steam, sprinkled water and brown paint sprayed over part of the panel.

06 · In the field

I took it to the people
who run the buses.

Indore's city buses are run by AICTSL, and their Integrated Command and Control Centre watches the whole fleet. I asked Mr. Roshan Agrawal, Director of Chartered Speed, how drivers talk to each other on the road. Then I demonstrated the prototype on his desk.

Can bus drivers talk to each other on the road?
“No. They can only do so by stopping the bus. Walkie-talkies are expensive and inefficient.”
If a passenger's hand is out, or luggage is falling, can the bus behind warn the one in front?
“No. The only way is to call the service centre, which then informs the bus.”
Would you put ViCAS in your buses?
“Yes, we would definitely want to. Once it's two-way, we'd take it to the head of our company.”

“This is a completely new concept. We had never thought of something like this.”

Mr. Roshan Agrawal, after the demonstration

07 · Cost

The whole thing:
₹1,874

Systems that let vehicles talk exist abroad, but they lean on Wi‑Fi and costly hardware that pushes up the price of a car. That doesn't work for most of India, where the signal on the edge of a city is patchy or gone.

ViCAS needs no network at all. Every part is off the shelf, and the add-on is simple enough to fit to a car, a two-wheeler or a truck with little mechanical experience.

  • No Wi‑Fi, SIM or data
  • No licence needed for light
  • Off-the-shelf parts

08 · Down the road

Where it goes next.

The prototype sends one way, in voice. Each of these is a milestone on from there.

  1. ViCAS
    01

    Two-way

    A light on the back and a sensor on the front, so the car ahead can answer. The digital Li‑Fi sketches are the first step.

  2. ViCAS
    02

    Headlights as the LED

    Send through the lights every vehicle already has, instead of adding one.

  3. ViCAS
    03

    Text for deaf drivers

    Show the message on the car's screen, for drivers who can't hear a horn at all.

  4. ViCAS
    04

    On the windscreen

    A screen below the windscreen at 45° reflects the message onto the glass.

  5. ViCAS
    05

    Clear the way

    Coded priority messages for ambulances, police and fire engines.

  6. ViCAS
    06

    Part of ADAS

    Join the driver-assistance systems cars are getting for the same reason: fewer accidents.

  7. ViCAS
    07

    Drivers and driverless

    Give human drivers and driverless cars a shared, exact way to warn each other.

  8. ViCAS
    08

    Silent and off-grid

    Convoys that must stay undetected, forest patrols that mustn't disturb wildlife, and roads with no network.
